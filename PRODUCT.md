## Product Purpose

Help established service businesses serve more clients well without every
increase in business requiring another coordinator, another subscription, or
another hour of the owner's evening. Create room for growth, healthier margins,
and valuable work by reducing operational toil and personal intervention.
Lower software spend is a supporting benefit.

This application has three surfaces: a public marketing site, an internal Admin,
and a customer-facing project site.

The marketing, branding, and offer are being rebuilt in full. The former Astro
site is discarded product context: its copy, audience, positioning, offer,
engagement terms, claims, and visual identity must not be carried forward.
Future work must use the new decisions recorded here.

The owner's full existing essay archive at `https://www.birdcar.dev/writing/`
will be republished and may be studied for written voice. Preserve the essays'
text, titles, original dates, URLs, and links. Typography and figures may be
art-directed for the new brand, preserving chart data, labels, and meaning.
The posts do not restore any other former-site copy, marketing, positioning,

## Users

- Marketing buyers: owners and operational leaders of established, client-facing
  service businesses. DFW and Texas, Oklahoma, Arkansas, and Louisiana are the
  geographic priorities. Roughly 10–100 employees is an initial targeting guide,
  not a hard eligibility rule.
- Agencies, professional-services firms, and local service or trade businesses

  a functioning business with customers and good people depends on too much
  remembering, chasing, copying, and owner intervention.
- A good client cares about the business and its people, can make decisions, has
  a budget for meaningful improvements, and provides access to the people doing
  the work. They can show what hurts without having to diagnose the process or
  specify software themselves.
- Admin: the people administering the application.
- Customer project site: customers commissioning and following project work.
  Their specific permission roles remain to be defined.

## Positioning

Take responsibility for understanding an operational problem, implementing the
right intervention, and helping people use and maintain the result. The work
combines operational problem-solving, software delivery, and ongoing care.

The owner's strengths are recognizing patterns in messy work, distinguishing
causes from symptoms, explaining systems without condescension, and connecting
technical decisions to the experience of employees and customers. Custom tools
and workflows should fit the client's business and customers. AI is one possible
implementation tool; it is not the proposition.

The owner's outcome chain (2026-09-25): tools built for the client's work are
the how; better work, faster is the key outcome and leads marketing; growing
without hiring is the key benefit that outcome enables; healthier margins are
the eventual result. Birdcar is not an AI agency.

## Operating Context

The buying moment often occurs when a previously manageable process stops being
manageable. Examples include:

- Considering another administrative hire because the existing team is overloaded.
- Onboarding or follow-up that depends on someone remembering every step.
- Reporting assembled manually from five or more sources.
- Missed follow-ups, late invoices, and owners catching failures after hours.
- An employee's departure exposing essential knowledge held only in their head.

Prospects may already pay for many overlapping tools or have experimented with
AI. Those tools can help locally while adding complexity across the whole
workflow. Diagnosis must include how people and systems actually work together.

## Offer and Working Relationship

The default entry is The Walkthrough: a free, roughly one-hour conversation,
followed within three business days by a written report describing the observed
problems and recommended improvements. The conversation is a video call by
default, or in person when the prospect prefers. The prospect keeps the report and can hire
the owner to implement it, use it independently, or walk away without an
implementation purchase.

Booking uses the owner's Cal.com event `https://cal.com/birdcar/walkthrough`
(namespace `walkthrough`). The marketing page explains the offer and sends
visitors to that event to select and confirm a time. Every booking action on
the site leads to the Walkthrough page, where visitors answer three fit checks
before the calendar appears (owner decision, 2026-09-25): customers and people
doing the work; one process that takes too much chasing, copying, or
remembering; and whether I can talk to the people who do it. Only a "Not yet"
on that last check stops booking. More qualification may be added later. A
custom integration is not required for launch. The booking page draws offer
structure from `https://www.acquisition.com/workshop`: recognizable problem, concrete take-away,
process, objections, and clear invitations, written in the owner's voice.
A VSL may be added later; there is no video placeholder at launch.

An alternative paid entry buys approximately 5–10 total hours of the owner's
time across one calendar week for deeper discovery with the team, its processes,
and its pains. The usual pattern is one focused hour per day with one or more
team members, via Zoom/Meet or in person, plus report preparation. The report
typically takes two hours to produce, with five hours as the intended maximum.
It details modernization opportunities, how to implement them, and which tools
to use. The client can commission implementation or use the report themselves.

Both entries produce a useful report. Their difference is discovery depth and
recommendation detail; the free report arrives within three business days, while
the precise boundaries of each report and the paid report's turnaround remain
open. Implementation is a separate purchase in either path.

A clearly scoped implementation engagement around an expensive or frustrating
workflow follows this approach:

1. Understand the workflow with the people doing the work.
2. Agree on what improvement would mean.
3. Build and test the intervention with its users.
4. Leave a working system, documentation, and training.

The owner's planning expectation is roughly eight weeks or less for a custom
software delivery. Establish the schedule for each scope; this is not a universal
delivery guarantee.

Implementation can include integrations, internal tools, client portals,
automated reporting and follow-up, sales-funnel design, and repeatable
AI-assisted workflows with appropriate human review. The scope follows the
problem; these are delivery capabilities, not a requirement to sell every tool
or service in every engagement.

Ongoing care can cover maintenance, failures, new-staff adoption, improvements,
and discovery of further opportunities as the business changes. Paid workshops
and training can stand alone or start a longer relationship.

The retainer includes a one-hour weekly check-in meeting for questions, bug
reports, and discussion. Implementation, maintenance, directing agents, and
reviewing their work require additional time beyond that meeting. Whether that
additional work is included in the retainer price or charged separately remains
open. Development agents accelerate delivery, as with DataDash.

The owner expects an initial period of fixes and changes after deployment,
followed by lighter ongoing work, primarily package updates and maintenance.
This expectation rests on software tailored to the client's needs and relatively
stable business processes; it is a planning assumption about future workload.

## Product Principles

- Judge improvements by the actual workflow and its users. Agree on success with
  the client rather than advertising arbitrary productivity promises.
- Create capacity for people to do valuable work. Exclude employee surveillance
  and projects whose entire purpose is replacing people regardless of consequences.
- Make adoption part of delivery through user involvement, documentation, and
  training. Avoid building systems nobody has time to adopt or offering AI theater.
- Earn recurring work through continued value and care. Clients must be able to
  leave; dependency on the provider is not the retention mechanism.
- Keep work and support bounded. Avoid an undefined arrangement covering
  anything technical at any time.

## Written Voice

The owner's instructions and corrections are authoritative. The following
working interpretation comes from the essays linked below, read for voice only.
It should be refined by the owner's response to actual copy.

- Start from something recognizable: a moment at work, a frustrating repetition,
  or a specific observation. Develop the reasoning until the conclusion feels earned.
- Speak directly to the reader. Use contractions, ordinary words, occasional
  asides, and varied sentence lengths. Give a developed thought room to breathe;
  do not reduce every paragraph to a sequence of advertising fragments.
- Make the point of view visible. Explain what I notice, what I think is wrong,
  how I approach it, and why it matters to the people involved. Expertise shows
  through specific reasoning and responsibility for the work.
- Keep the dry humor and candor, including the ability to admit a mistake or
  revise a belief. Do not manufacture certainty, provocation, or profanity to
  imitate a personality. The criticism targets assumptions and broken systems;
  it does not belittle the person asking for help.
- Treat useful technology with curiosity and skepticism. Describe its actual
  contribution and limits; let the human consequences explain why the work matters.
- Keep offers and actions easy to understand. Hormozi supplies commercial
  structure, not a substitute personality or a vocabulary of hype and urgency.

Voice references:

- [Your metrics are bullshit](https://www.birdcar.dev/writing/your-metrics-are-bullshit/):
  recognizable working scenes, direct criticism, and concern for the people
  affected by a system.
- [Just build it twice](https://www.birdcar.dev/writing/just-build-it-twice/):
  first-person reasoning, conversational asides, and a conclusion developed from
  experience rather than an inflated productivity claim.
- [Your AI wrote a bug](https://www.birdcar.dev/writing/your-ai-wrote-a-bug/):
  concrete failure, personal accountability, and self-aware humor.

Use these as style evidence only. Do not import their anecdotes or numbers as
client results, copy the old marketing, or mechanically reproduce their sentence
patterns. New copy must express the offer and constraints established here.
