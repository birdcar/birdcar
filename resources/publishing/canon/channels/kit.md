# Distribution kit channel

A distribution kit is three short pieces cut from one approved blog post: a LinkedIn post, a Bluesky thread and an email blurb. Each one carries one idea from the post to a place an owner already reads, then sends that owner to the post. Everything is written in the public register of voice/core.md: sentence case, first-person singular, no politics, no profanity, no dark humor, no emoji and no exclamation points. Nick copies each piece out by hand and posts it himself; nothing is posted automatically.

## What every kit piece shares

- The approved post is the only source. Use only what input.flagship.text says, and state factual claims only as input.flagship.claim_manifest states them. Never add a claim, a number, a client, a result, a quotation or an anecdote the post does not carry.
- One idea per piece: a scene, a reframing or the post's answer, cut from the post. Never a summary of the whole post.
- The link to the post is input.flagship.canonical_url, written out in full, exactly once.
- Never link to the Walkthrough. The invitation lives in the post, after the reader has the answer.
- Never link to a post before it is published. Nick posts a kit piece only after the post is live, so write the link as if it is live.
- No engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment below"), no hype hook, no urgency and no scarcity.

## LinkedIn post

- Front-load the target phrase: input.flagship.target_query belongs in the first sentence, because about 210 characters show before "more" and the first words become the post's slug.
- 3,000 characters is the ceiling; most posts should be far shorter.
- Write paragraphs, not broetry: never one sentence per line.
- No link in the post text. The first comment holds the link to the post, with at most one short sentence before it.
- At most three plain topic hashtags, only at the very end. None is fine.

## Bluesky thread

- Each post is at most 300 characters, counting its number.
- Number every post i/n at its start ("1/4"), where n is the number of posts in the thread.
- Each post reads on its own and is clearly part of the series: the first post opens with the claim or the scene, the middle posts develop it, and the last post lands the point and carries the link to the post.
- Sentence case and the public register. Bluesky's casual habits (lowercase hot takes, profanity, one-liners without setup) do not apply here.
- Thread only as far as the idea needs; three to five posts is the usual shape.

## Email blurb

- A subject line of at most 50 characters, plain and specific, never clickbait.
- One preview line: the sentence an inbox shows after the subject.
- A body of two to four sentences: the claim from the post, why it matters to an owner, and the link to the post.
- The blurb links to the post only.
