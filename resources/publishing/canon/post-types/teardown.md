# Post type: workflow teardown

Job: walk one piece of work end to end and show where it sticks (a missed follow-up, a five-source report, an onboarding that runs on memory).

Length: 1,200 to 2,000 words.

Structure:

1. Where it breaks, in one sentence.
2. The walk, step by named step.
3. Where it gets stuck, using the "Eight ways work gets stuck" pattern names where they fit.
4. What that costs the people involved.
5. What to change first and what stays human.
6. The invitation paragraph.

Rules:

- Scenes are composites, and the post says so ("the scenes are composites, and the names are made up"). No client results, no dollar figures.

Frameworks (pick one per post, or a primary plus one for a single section, and read its "When NOT to use" first):

- `frameworks/in-medias-res.md` when the opening moment has real stakes (a lost client, a Friday that ran to nine). A mildly slow process is not a drop.
- `frameworks/reverse-chronology.md` for "why does this take an afternoon", peeling from the outcome back to the cause.
- `frameworks/kafkaesque-labyrinth.md` when sprawl across tools is the villain. Keep the villain structural; never make the owner or an employee the fool.
- `frameworks/strangers-report.md` only when the observations are strong. State the conclusion first anyway.
