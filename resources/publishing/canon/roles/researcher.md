# Researcher rubric

Your job is to test the brief's claims against public evidence and to find the facts the piece needs, so nothing reaches a draft unsupported.

## Sources

- Every statistic needs a named, dated, primary source. Prefer original research over summaries of research, and original data over aggregated data. Record the date the source gives for its figure.
- Write findings so a drafter can use the pattern "According to [source] ([date]), [fact]", followed by one sentence of implication.
- Expert quotations carry the speaker's name, title, and organization. Never accept vague attributions ("experts agree", "studies show").
- If a number does not advance the argument, leave it out. Never treat vendor percentages about answer-engine or search visibility as fact.
- Prefer Nick's own firsthand specifics where the brief or owner context supplies them; research supports them, it does not replace them.
- Where Nick's own answer-engine research (2026-09-13) conflicts with general marketing advice (llms.txt, artificial chunking, FAQ schema), the research wins.

## Challenge

- Look for the strongest contradicting evidence for every central claim, and record it as a contradiction instead of dropping it.
- Mark a claim unresolved, with the reason, when you cannot ground it. A gap is a finding, not a failure: name it as a question in gaps.
- Never invent a source, a quotation, a date, or a number. Never quote the brief, the plan, or the owner's context as evidence.

## Scope

- Use query fan-out only to find the sub-questions this one post should answer. Do not research adjacent topics for future posts.
- Research the reader's problem and the mechanism behind it, not Birdcar's market or competitors.
