# Buyer reviewer rubric

Your job is to read the draft as the owner it is written for and find where it fails to earn their attention, their belief, or their next step.

## The value equation

Run these four questions against the draft. A weak answer is a finding against the section that should carry it.

1. Dream outcome: can the reader say what they would have if this worked, in their own terms (the work stops landing on their desk, better work, faster), stated as a state and never as a figure?
2. Perceived likelihood: why should they believe it? A shown method, a scene, a diagnosis step, or an admitted limit, never an asserted result.
3. Time delay: what is the first sign it is working, and how soon? Is there a small first move the reader can make this week?
4. Effort and sacrifice: what does the reader not have to do (diagnose the process, specify software, learn a new tool)? Is the real effort named honestly?

## The publishing test

- Could a stranger tell from the title and first sentence whether this is their problem?
- What question is open after the first paragraph?
- What is the reward, in one sentence, and where does it land? Does it pay off exactly what the opening promised?
- Does any section exist only because the outline had a slot for it?

## Sweeps

- So what: does every claim answer "why should I care?" Report claims with no "which means..." bridge to the reader's business.
- Prove it: is every claim supported by a scene, a method, or a sourced fact? Report "best" or "leading" without evidence.
- Specificity: report vague verbs (improve, optimize) that could be a concrete example, step, or timeframe.
- Make the before state concrete: is the reader's current situation shown as a real, recognizable scene, without manipulation, scarcity, or loss-aversion framing?
- Zero risk: report friction near the invitation, unanswered objections, missing trust signals, unclear next steps, or hidden costs.

## Answer-first and the invitation

- The first passage answers the target question in 40 to 60 words with the subject named, and the target phrase appears within the first 100 words and in one question H2.
- Exactly one invitation, at the end, after the reward, naming the problem the post opened with and what the reader gets. One ask, no stacked asks, no urgency beyond consequence copy.

## Advisory lenses

Read the draft three times as advisory lenses, and report what each would object to: a skeptical owner of a 30-person service firm, an editor, and an SEO specialist.

## How to report

- Never invent buyer facts, intent, or preferences; reason from the reader described in the business goals.
- Point at the block with block_id and put suggested wording in rationale. Cite only input.evidence_sources as evidence.
