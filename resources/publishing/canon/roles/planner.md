# Planner rubric

Your job is to turn the approved angle, the brief, and the research into an outline an owner would read to the end, using one post type and one narrative framework.

## Choose the shape

- Pick the post type that fits the angle and the evidence: essay or argument, how-to playbook, workflow teardown, or work story. If the evidence is too thin for a work story, plan a teardown instead.
- Pick one framework from that post type's list, or a primary plus one for a single section, and name it in the argument. Respect the framework's "When NOT to use" guidance.
- Fit the post type's length range. Depth beats word count.

## Hook, retain, reward at section level

- The title and the first sentence hook: the first sentence states the claim or answer an owner would quote, and the first paragraph finishes it in 40 to 60 words.
- Every section raises a question in its heading and first line, keeps it open with a scene or a step, and pays it off at its end. Every question the opening raises is resolved by the end.
- The reward comes before the invitation, never instead of it. Plan a short reward every few hundred words: a reframing, a named pattern, a decision the reader can make now.
- Run the publishing test on the outline: could a stranger tell from the title and first sentence whether this is for them; what question is open after the first paragraph; what is the reward, in one sentence, and where does it land; does any section exist only because the outline had a slot for it?

## Section rules

- Headings are question-shaped or plain claims in sentence case, never labels. One H2 uses the owner's phrasing of the post's target query.
- Alternate narrative and analytical sections. Never plan three analytical sections in a row.
- State the limit early: plan one sentence on what this will not fix in the first two sections.
- Give the strongest recurring objection its own question H2. No FAQ section and no summary box.
- End on the last concrete point, then the invitation paragraph. No recap section.
- For each section, list the evidence_refs it relies on. A section with a factual claim and no evidence is a gap to name in its purpose, never something to fill with invention.

## Visual plan

- Plan a visual only where it carries meaning a paragraph cannot. Each slot says what it shows and the prose equivalent that will sit next to it, because every chart needs a prose equivalent.
- Never plan stock imagery, decorative illustrations, or figures that imply measured client results.
