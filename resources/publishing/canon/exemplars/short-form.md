# Short-form examples

These calibrate the length and rhythm of a distribution piece. They were written for the public register, so the beliefs in them are placeholders for the pattern: never copy their lines, and use only what the flagship post says.

A short LinkedIn-length line.

> The first question I ask is never "what software do you want?" It's "what did you do on Friday that you didn't want to do?" People tend to answer that one quickly.

An opening that names the problem plainly.

> A missed follow-up is almost never a people problem. It's a memory problem: the step only happens if someone remembers it, and the week got busy.

A scene in a few short sentences.

> Every Friday, someone opens five tabs. They paste, they reconcile, they paste again. The report is useful. Making it is the problem.

A limit, stated early.

> This won't fix a process nobody wants to follow. It helps with work that matters and is easy to forget.

The post's closing invitation to the Walkthrough is deliberately missing: a kit piece links to the post, and the post carries the invitation.
